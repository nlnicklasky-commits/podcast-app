# PodBrain: Intellectual Property & Content Rights Assessment

## 1. Copyright Ownership Analysis

**Podcast audio** is copyrighted by the creator or their production company upon fixation. Publishing via RSS does not transfer copyright or grant reproduction rights beyond what is necessary for standard podcast consumption (streaming/downloading for personal listening). The `process-podcast` edge function downloads the full audio file from `enclosure_url` (line 293-320 in `index.ts`), temporarily stores it in Supabase Storage, then deletes it after transcription (line 667-669). While transient, this constitutes reproduction of copyrighted material.

**Transcripts** generated by Whisper are derivative works of the original audio. Copyright in the underlying spoken content belongs to the podcast creator. The transcript itself -- as a mechanical reproduction of the words spoken -- does not generate new copyright for PodBrain. Permanently storing `full_text` in the `transcripts` table (line 503-508) creates a persistent copy of copyrighted expression.

**AI-generated insights** (summaries, topics, key points, entities) produced by the LLM at lines 586-654 are more transformative. However, they are still derived from copyrighted source material. The degree of transformation determines whether they constitute infringing derivative works or permissible fair use outputs.

## 2. Fair Use Assessment

Under 17 U.S.C. Section 107, four factors govern fair use:

| Factor | Personal Use | Productized (Multi-User) |
|--------|-------------|--------------------------|
| **Purpose & character** | Private research/study -- favors fair use | Commercial service -- weakens claim substantially |
| **Nature of work** | Factual/informational content -- mildly favors fair use | Same |
| **Amount used** | Entire works transcribed and stored -- weighs against | Same, at scale |
| **Market effect** | Minimal for one user -- neutral | Could substitute for listening, reducing downloads/ad revenue -- strongly against |

**Personal use verdict:** Likely defensible. Personal transcription for note-taking has strong fair use precedent (Sony v. Universal, 1984 -- time-shifting for personal use). The RAG chat layer adds transformative purpose.

**Productized verdict:** Significantly weaker. Storing full transcripts of entire episodes for multiple users, enabling them to extract value without listening, directly impacts the market for the original. This is the pattern that made Google Books defensible only because it showed snippets, not full text (Authors Guild v. Google, 2015).

## 3. RSS Feed Legal Norms

Publishing an RSS feed with an `<enclosure>` tag is an implicit license for podcast apps to **download audio for personal playback**. This is well-established industry practice and the entire purpose of RSS syndication.

However, this implied license does **not** extend to:
- Transcribing the audio content into text
- Creating derivative text works (summaries, insights)
- Storing full-text reproductions in a searchable database
- Serving that content to third-party users

The Podcast Index API terms (`podcastindex.org`) grant access to feed metadata and URLs, not rights to the underlying content. PodBrain's `User-Agent: PodcastBrain/1.0` header (line 299) correctly identifies itself, but downloading for transcription exceeds the scope of the implied RSS license.

## 4. YouTube/Cobalt Risks

**Risk level: HIGH.** Extracting audio from YouTube via Cobalt (lines 324-403) directly violates YouTube's Terms of Service, Section 5(B): "You are not allowed to... access, reproduce, download... any part of the Service or any Content except as expressly authorized." This is not a gray area.

Key exposures:
- **DMCA Section 1201:** Cobalt circumvents YouTube's access controls. Circumvention tools carry statutory damages of up to $2,500 per act.
- **Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA):** Automated extraction from YouTube could constitute unauthorized access.
- **Google enforcement:** Google actively pursues injunctions against YouTube download services (e.g., RIAA v. youtube-dl, 2020).

The CLAUDE.md describes YouTube as "fallback only" and Podcast Index as "legally clean" -- this distinction is accurate and should be formalized by removing the YouTube/Cobalt path before productization.

## 5. Derivative Works Analysis

Under 17 U.S.C. Section 101, a derivative work is one "based upon one or more preexisting works." PodBrain creates three tiers:

1. **Transcripts** (`full_text`): Near-verbatim reproduction. Clearly derivative. Minimal transformation.
2. **Chunks + Embeddings**: Text chunks retain copyrighted expression. Embeddings (numerical vectors) are sufficiently transformed that they likely do not constitute copyrightable expression themselves -- but they enable reconstruction of meaning from copyrighted text.
3. **Insights** (summaries, key points, entities): Most transformative. Short factual summaries of ideas/facts are generally not copyrightable (Feist v. Rural, 1991 -- facts are not copyrightable). However, summaries that closely paraphrase original creative expression could infringe.

The primary risk sits with full transcript storage. Embeddings alone would be significantly safer from a copyright perspective.

## 6. DMCA Considerations

If PodBrain is productized as a hosted service, it becomes a "service provider" under DMCA Section 512. Obligations include:

- **Designated DMCA agent** registered with the U.S. Copyright Office
- **Takedown procedure**: Accept and process valid DMCA notices, remove infringing content "expeditiously"
- **Counter-notice process**: Allow users to dispute takedowns
- **Repeat infringer policy**: Terminate accounts of repeat infringers
- **No actual knowledge**: Safe harbor only applies if PodBrain does not have actual knowledge of infringement -- but since the system itself creates the copies, this defense is weak

Currently, none of these protections are in place. There is no copyright notice, no DMCA contact, and no takedown mechanism.

## 7. Recommended Protections

**Immediate (before any public launch):**
- Remove the YouTube/Cobalt extraction path entirely for any productized version
- Add prominent attribution in the UI: podcast name, episode title, creator, and a direct link to the original episode
- Add a copyright notice and terms of service to the site
- Store only chunks or summaries rather than full verbatim transcripts if possible -- this dramatically improves the fair use position
- Add a `robots.txt`-equivalent signal: respect podcast-level opt-out flags (e.g., `<podcast:locked>` tag in RSS 2.0 namespace)

**Before productization:**
- Register a DMCA designated agent with the Copyright Office
- Implement a takedown request workflow (accept notices, remove content, notify user)
- Draft Terms of Service that disclaim ownership of podcast content and establish user responsibility
- Consider licensing: some podcast networks (Spotify, iHeart) offer API access with explicit transcription rights
- Implement transcript access controls: show snippets in search results rather than full text, similar to Google Books

**Ongoing:**
- Monitor the Podcast Index `<podcast:transcript>` tag -- when creators publish their own transcripts, using those (as PodBrain already does for `transcript_url` at line 217) is legally safer than generating unauthorized ones
- Track evolving case law on AI training and content reproduction (NYT v. OpenAI, ongoing as of 2025)
